Start seine and it opens a live dashboard right away: how fast you are hashing, how many shares the pool accepted, what you are owed, how tuning is going, and a log of what it does. This tour goes panel by panel through seine v0.3.1, explains what each label means and where its number comes from, and covers the keys you can use while you mine.

![seine v0.3.1's terminal dashboard mining on bntpool.com: current hashrate 16.63 H/s on 21 CPU threads, tuning done, 222 shares accepted and 1 rejected, the pool balance with a 1 BNT minimum payout, and a log of accepted shares](/assets/blog/seine-dashboard-v031.webp "seine's dashboard in v0.3.1")

The picture is seine v0.3.1's own dashboard code rendered at 120 × 26 characters with demo values: a Ryzen 9 9950X at its measured 16.63 H/s on 21 threads, two hours into a session on bntpool.com. The address is shortened and fictional. It's the reference for the rest of this post.

## What does seine's dashboard show?

<Answer>
Seine's dashboard shows your live and average hashrate, completed hashes, chain height and network hashrate, the miner's state, your share difficulty with rejected and accepted shares, the hashrate of each device, your pool, thread count and tuning status, your bntpool.com balance, and a log you can scroll back through. It refreshes about once a second.
</Answer>

From top to bottom, the panels are the header, a row of three stat panels (`HASHRATE`, `NETWORK`, `MINING`), `DEVICES`, `CONFIG`, `POOL BALANCE` and `LOG`. On a wide terminal, `CONFIG` and the balance panel sit side by side.

The dashboard opens before mining starts. On the first run, seine asks for your payout address and pool inside the dashboard, then shows hardware checks and startup progress on screen, so you can follow each step.

## The header: version, waves and updates

The header's title shows the seine version, here `seine v0.3.1`. When a newer release is out, it shows `update vX.Y.Z available`, then counts down to the automatic install: `updating to vX.Y.Z in 42s`, with `u now  s skip` beside it. Press `u` to install at once or `s` to skip that version. Mining continues during the countdown, and the [update guide](/blog/update-seine-miner/) explains what happens next.

Under the title, two rows of waves drift past one step per second. Every time the pool accepts one of your shares, or your rig finds a block, a gold diamond lands on the top row at the right edge and scrolls left one column per second. On a terminal about 120 columns wide, the diamonds you can see are the last two minutes or so of accepted work. `uptime` sits at the end of the bottom row.

## How does seine measure hashrate?

The `HASHRATE` panel has three lines:

- **Current** is your hashrate over a rolling five-second window. A hash here is one complete 2 GiB Argon2id computation, so H/s counts finished hashes per second. NVIDIA GPUs finish work in bursts, so their current rate uses a 30-second window to stay steady.
- **Average** is every hash this session divided by the time spent mining. Time spent waiting, for example for a node to sync or after an invalid address, is left out.
- **Total** is the number of hashes completed since you started seine.

In the picture, Current reads 16.63 H/s and Average 16.41 H/s. Average starts lower because it includes the first minutes, when every thread is finishing its first hash and tuning is still choosing the thread count. The longer seine runs, the closer the two get.

## Network, state and shares

The `NETWORK` panel shows the chain `Height` you are mining on and the `Global` network hashrate, which seine reads from bntpool.com's public stats in pool mode.

`State` tells you what the miner is doing. Green `working` is the one you want to see. In pool mode you will also see these during startup and reconnects:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `pool-connecting` | Opening the Stratum connection |
| `pool-connected` | Connected, logging in |
| `pool-authenticated` | Login accepted, waiting for the first job |
| `working` | Hashing on the current job |
| `pool-disconnected` | Connection dropped; seine reconnects on its own |
| `pool-login-rejected` | The pool refused the login; the log gives the reason |

Solo mining against your own node adds states such as `daemon-syncing` and `stale-tip`. While seine is waiting on something, Current drops to zero so it never shows a rate it isn't producing.

The `MINING` panel counts shares for this session:

- **Difficulty:** the share difficulty the pool set for this rig.
- **Rejected:** shares the pool did not accept.
- **Accepted:** shares the pool credited to you.

bntpool.com adjusts each rig's share difficulty so it sends a steady stream of shares whatever its hashrate, so a faster rig gets a higher difficulty. Your payout follows accepted shares. In daemon mode the panel shows `Submitted`, `Stale` and `Accepted` instead, counting solutions sent to your own node.

## Devices, config and pool balance

`DEVICES` lists each mining backend with its current and average hashrate: `cpu#1` for the CPU, then `nvidia#1`, `amd#1` and so on for each GPU. GPUs start in the background while the CPU already mines, and a card that is still starting shows its progress until it comes online.

`CONFIG` shows how this run is set up:

- **Pool** is the pool address, `bntpool.com:3333` by default, or `Daemon` with your node's API address in daemon mode.
- **Threads** is the number of CPU mining threads. When the keys are available, `[+] [-]` appears beside it. **Worker** is the rig name this machine logs in to the pool with.
- **Tuning** shows automatic CPU tuning: what it is comparing and how long it has left, then `done`. The [CPU tuning guide](/blog/seine-automatic-cpu-tuning/) explains every state.

To give a rig a recognizable name on the pool, pass it at startup:

```sh
./seine --pool-worker rig-01
```

`POOL BALANCE` reads your balance from bntpool.com every few seconds. `Pending` is what you have earned and not yet been paid, including estimated rewards from blocks that are still maturing, `Paid` is what the pool has sent to your address, and `Miner` on the right is your address shortened to its first and last six characters. When you mine solo on the pool, with `solo:` in front of your address, that label reads `Solo`. `Min payout` shows the pool's minimum payout, 1 BNT on bntpool.com. In daemon mode this panel becomes `WALLET`, with your node wallet's `Pending` and `Unlocked` balances.

## Reading the log

`LOG` lists what seine is doing, newest at the bottom, each line with a tag and how long ago it happened. Repeated lines fold into one with a count, such as `share accepted ×104`, so the log stays readable over a long session. The tags you will see most on a pool are:

- `CONN`: the pool connection, such as `connected to pool`
- `SHARE`: `share accepted`, or a rejection with its reason
- `CPU`: tuning results, such as `tuned to 21 threads`
- `BACKEND`: devices starting, such as `cpu: starting 21 threads (~42.0 GiB RAM)`

The log shows what you need by default. Add `--verbose` to see internal settings and routine activity such as each new job.

## Which keys work in the dashboard?

<Answer>
Press + or = to add a CPU thread and - to remove one, once the Tuning row reads done. Use the arrow keys, PgUp, PgDn and Home to scroll back through the log, and End to follow new lines again. During an update countdown, u installs now and s skips that version. Press q or Ctrl+C to quit.
</Answer>

Thread changes apply to the running CPU threads at once, while GPUs and the pool connection keep running. While tuning is still choosing a count, the keys wait and the dashboard says how long tuning has left. A key press lasts for that run only; the next start uses your saved tuning again. For a fixed count from the start, use `--threads`.

While you scroll back, the log's title reads `LOG  paused, End to follow` and the view holds still until you press `End`.

## Small terminals and plain output

In a terminal narrower than 80 columns, the three stat panels stack vertically. In a short window, seine switches to a compact view that keeps Threads, State, Current and Average on screen above the log. The default `--ui auto` shows the dashboard only in an interactive terminal; `--ui plain` gives a plain scrolling log for servers, log files and process managers.
